# Ksheer's report contribution (integration draft)

We prepared 4,180 completed EPL fixtures from football-data.co.uk, covering 2015–16 through 2025–26 (380 per season), with no core-field exclusions. COVID-delayed July 2020 fixtures remain in 2019–20. The shared dataset separates Bet365 pre-closing decimal odds from multiplicatively de-vigged probabilities, p_k=(1/o_k)/Σ_j(1/o_j). Optional bookmaker and closing quotes retain their source identifiers. Goals and results are evaluation targets; closing quotes cannot enter an opening-price strategy. The provider snapshots are not verified first-ever opening prices or synchronized trade opportunities.

**Calibration.** The market's unscaled multiclass Brier score was 0.5690; log loss was 0.9602 nats. Reliability diagrams summarize H/D/A separately, with season-cluster intervals. Joint logistic intercept/slope diagnostics are retrospective descriptions, not fitted forecasting gains. Full coefficients and bin counts appear in the experiment documentation.

**Mispricing.** We tested mean observed-minus-implied probability residuals across five fixed probability ranges, three outcome types and eligible teams (at least 100 fixtures and five seasons). Season-cluster sandwich uncertainty preserves dependence among outcomes within a match and within seasons. A single Benjamini–Hochberg family covered all 28 eligible tests; **0 survived q<.05**. Date-cluster and arbitrary-dependence BY sensitivities are reported. This does not establish market efficiency or rule out smaller effects: only eleven season clusters are available, and probability deviations need not translate into profits after margin.

**Luck versus skill.** A pre-specified always-home baseline returned -3.88% historically. Keeping selections and odds fixed, 10,000 simulations drew one categorical outcome per match under de-vigged market probabilities; all bets on a fixture share that outcome. The market-null expected ROI was -4.29%; its 95% outcome-simulation interval was [-8.46%, -0.10%]. The observed return lay at percentile 57.5 (upper-tail Monte Carlo p=0.425). This conditions on an assumed market model and independent matches, not known true probabilities. Separately, a date-block historical bootstrap gave a mean-ROI 95% CI [-8.07%, 0.27%]; this confidence interval answers a different question from the outcome-simulation interval.

**Power.** Using the empirical home-odds distribution, a one-sided 5% test of positive net ROI required the following independent-bet counts for 80% CLT power: 1%: 119,546; 2%: 29,969; 3%: 13,356; 5%: 4,834. Monte Carlo checks at these counts are documented. At a true 0% ROI, no finite sample reaches 80% power. Hypothetical edges are constructed design assumptions, not discovered strategies. The break-even power null (q=1/o) differs from the negative-return de-vigged market null; dependence reduces effective sample size.

These analyses provide market diagnostics and inference tools for Person A's independent modeling and chronological backtests. They neither demonstrate a profitable strategy nor prove bookmakers unbeatable.
